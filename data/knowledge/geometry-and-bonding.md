# VSEPR geometry
source: Gillespie & Hargittai, The VSEPR Model of Molecular Geometry (1991); IUPAC Gold Book
Valence-shell electron-pair repulsion (VSEPR) predicts shape from the number of electron domains (bonded atoms plus lone pairs) around a central atom. Two domains are linear (180°), three trigonal planar (120°), four tetrahedral (109.5°), five trigonal bipyramidal (90° and 120°), six octahedral (90°). Lone pairs occupy more space than bonding pairs, so they compress the remaining angles: ammonia (AX3E) is trigonal pyramidal with H–N–H about 107°, and water (AX2E2) is bent with H–O–H about 104.5°. Multiple bonds count as one domain.

# Hybridization
source: Pauling, The Nature of the Chemical Bond (1960)
Hybridization is a bookkeeping model linking the number of electron domains to orbital mixing: sp (2 domains, linear), sp2 (3 domains, trigonal planar), sp3 (4 domains, tetrahedral). Carbon in methane is sp3, in ethene sp2, in ethyne sp. Atoms whose lone pair is delocalised into a π system (amide nitrogen, pyrrole nitrogen, phenol oxygen) behave as sp2 and are planar.

# Octet rule and valence
source: IUPAC Gold Book; Housecroft & Sharpe, Inorganic Chemistry
Second-period elements (C, N, O, F) do not exceed eight valence electrons: carbon forms four bonds, nitrogen three (four as ammonium, N+), oxygen two (three as oxonium, O+), fluorine one. Hydrogen forms one bond. Elements of period three and beyond (P, S, Cl, Br, I, Xe) can show expanded valence, for example PF5, SF6 and XeF4. Formal charge = valence electrons − non-bonding electrons − ½ × bonding electrons.

# Aromaticity
source: IUPAC Gold Book; Hückel (1931)
A planar, cyclic, fully conjugated ring with 4n+2 π electrons (n = 0, 1, 2 …) is aromatic: benzene (6), pyridine (6), pyrrole (6, using the N lone pair), naphthalene (10). Aromatic rings are unusually stable and have equal C–C bond lengths near 1.39 Å. Rings with 4n π electrons that are planar and conjugated are antiaromatic.

# Typical bond lengths
source: Allen et al., J. Chem. Soc. Perkin Trans. 2 (1987) S1; CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics
C–H 1.09 Å; C–C 1.53 Å; C=C 1.33 Å; C≡C 1.20 Å; aromatic C–C 1.39 Å; C–O 1.43 Å; C=O 1.21 Å (1.16 Å in CO2); O–H 0.96 Å; N–H 1.01 Å; C–N 1.47 Å; C≡N 1.15 Å. Bond length shortens as bond order increases.
